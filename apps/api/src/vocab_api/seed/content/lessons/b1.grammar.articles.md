# Articles: a/an, the, zero article

Articles signal **how new or how known** a noun is to the listener. This is the
whole game. Ask yourself: *Is the listener meeting this noun for the first time,
or is it already identifiable?*

## The core contrast

- **a/an** — introducing *one instance of a category*: `I saw a dog.`
- **the** — pointing at *one specific thing already identifiable*: `I saw a dog. The dog was brown.`
- **zero article** — speaking about a category in general: `Dogs are loyal.`

`the` is not about the speaker alone; it is about what the **listener can also
identify**. If both of you know which thing you mean, you say `the`:

| First mention | Second mention |
|---|---|
| We booked **a** hotel. | **The** hotel was near the office. |

## Rules that catch non-native speakers

1. **Superlatives and unique things** always take `the`: *the best*, *the sun*,
   *the CEO* (in a given company).
2. **General plurals and uncountables** take no article: *Money doesn't grow on
   trees.* Not "The money" unless it's specific money.
3. **Jobs after "be"** take `a/an`: *She is an engineer.*
4. **Names of people, countries, cities** — zero article: *Maria is from Spain.*
   (Exceptions like *the USA, the UK* are idiomatic and must be memorised.)

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| I work for **a** startup. | I work for startup. |
| **The** meeting is at 10. | ~~A meeting~~ is at 10. (when both know which) |
| I like **∅** coffee. | I like the coffee. (general category) |

## Quick check

Would you write *a*, *the*, or nothing?

- *Could you open ___ window?* — **the** (we both know which one).
- *She bought ___ laptop yesterday.* — **a** (first mention).
- *___ technology changes fast.* — **∅** (general category).

The test comes down to identifiability. If in doubt, ask: *can the listener point
at it?* Yes → `the`; no → `a/an` (singular) or nothing (plural/uncountable).
