# Daily routine

Talking about your day is one of the first real conversations you have in a new
language. Learn the verbs in a natural order, then attach the time.

## The routine verbs

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| wake up | I **wake up** at seven. |
| get up | Then I **get up** and stretch. |
| have breakfast | I **have breakfast** at seven thirty. |
| go to work / school | I **go to work** by bus. |
| have lunch | We **have lunch** at one. |
| finish work | I **finish work** at six. |
| get home | I **get home** at half past six. |
| cook | I **cook** dinner. |
| go to bed | I **go to bed** at eleven. |

## Time phrases

- **at** + clock time: *at seven, at half past eight*.
- **in** + part of the day: *in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening*.
- **at night** (no *in*).

*I get up **at seven in the morning**. I go to bed **at night**.*

## Frequency adverbs

Put the adverb before the main verb: *always, usually, often, sometimes,
never*.

- *I **usually** have coffee first.*
- *She **never** watches TV in the morning.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| I wake **up** at six. | I wake at six up. |
| **at** seven | in seven |
| I **get home** at six. | I come to home at six. |

## Quick check

- *I ___ (get up) at seven.* → **get up**
- *She has lunch ___ one.* → **at**
- *He ___ (never / eat) breakfast.* → **never eats**
