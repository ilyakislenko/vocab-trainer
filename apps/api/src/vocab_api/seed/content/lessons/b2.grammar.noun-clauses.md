# Noun clauses and embedded questions

A noun clause works as **one noun** in the sentence: subject, object, or
complement. It opens with *that, whether/if, what, who, how, why*.

## Noun clauses as object

- *I believe **that the deadline matters**.*
- *We know **how it works**.*
- *Tell me **what you mean**.*

## Embedded questions — no inversion

Inside a noun clause the order is **statement order**, not question order:

- Direct: *Where does she work?*
- Embedded: *I asked **where she works**.* (no *does*)

The auxiliary disappears unless the clause is a *wh-* subject question:
*Do you know **what the problem is**?*

## Noun clauses as subject

- ***That she left** surprised everyone.*
- ***What we need** is more testing.*
- ***Whether it will work** is uncertain.*

A `that`-clause subject often moves to the end with `it` in front:
*It surprised everyone **that she left**.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| I don't know **where he is**. | I don't know where is he. (no inversion) |
| She asked **whether/if** it worked. | She asked that it worked. |
| **What matters** is quality. | That matters is quality. (what-clause as subject) |

## Common in the workplace

- *We need to decide **whether to proceed**.*
- *It's unclear **why the build failed**.*
- *Let's confirm **who owns the task**.*

## Quick check

- *I can't remember ___ I parked.* → **where**
- *___ the plan will work is unclear.* → **Whether**
- *Do you know ___ the server is?* → **where**

A noun clause is a noun wearing a sentence. Keep it in statement order and let it fill the noun slot.
