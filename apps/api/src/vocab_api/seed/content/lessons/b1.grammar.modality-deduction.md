# Modal verbs of deduction: must, can't, might

Deduction modals say **how sure you are** of a conclusion. They never change
form and are followed by a bare infinitive.

## The certainty scale

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| must | almost certain (logical) | The server is down — it **must** be overloaded. |
| can't / couldn't | almost certain it's NOT | He **can't** be at home; his car is here. |
| might / may / could | possible | It **might** rain this evening. |

`must` and `can't` are opposites, not synonyms: *must* asserts, *can't* denies.

## Past deduction

Add `have + V3`:

- `must have + V3` — near-certain past: *She must have missed the train.*
- `can't have + V3` — near-certain past negative: *He can't have seen the email.*
- `might have + V3` — possible past: *They might have left early.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| It **must** be cold outside. | It **must to** be cold. (no to) |
| He **can't have** forgotten. | He **mustn't have** forgotten. (mustn't = prohibition, not deduction) |
| She **might be** at the meeting. | She **might to be**… |

`mustn't` means *not allowed*: *You mustn't smoke here.* It is **not** the negative
of deduction — that's `can't`.

## Quick check

- *That ___ be the CEO — he's very senior.* → **must**
- *You ___ have seen it; you weren't there.* → **can't**
- *We ___ try the new API.* → **might**

Ask: am I asserting or denying? Assert → must; deny → can't; unsure → might. Past → add *have + V3*.
