# To be (am/is/are)

The verb **to be** is the first verb you need because it links a person or a
thing to a name, a job, a place, or an adjective. It has three present forms:
**am** (with *I*), **is** (with *he, she, it*), and **are** (with *you, we,
they*).

## The three forms

| Subject | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | I **am** Anna. |
| he / she / it | **is** | She **is** a doctor. |
| you / we / they | **are** | They **are** at work. |

Notice the short forms we use in speaking: *I'm, he's, she's, it's, you're,
we're, they're*.

## Making it negative

Add **not** after the verb: **am not, is not (isn't), are not (aren't)**.

- *I **am not** from Spain.*
- *He **isn't** a teacher.*
- *We **aren't** ready.*

## Asking questions

Move the verb before the subject:

- **Is** she your sister? → Yes, she **is**. / No, she **isn't**.
- **Are** they happy? → Yes, they **are**. / No, they **aren't**.

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| She **is** my friend. | She my friend. (the verb cannot be skipped) |
| I **am** 25. | I am 25 years. |
| **Are** you at home? | You are at home? (word order in questions) |

## Quick check

- *I ___ a student.* → **am**
- *Maria ___ from Italy.* → **is**
- *___ they at the office?* → **Are**
