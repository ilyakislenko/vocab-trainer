# Hedging and register

Hedging is **softening a claim** so it doesn't sound absolute or aggressive. In
professional English it protects relationships and makes you sound thoughtful,
not weak.

## Hedge with modals and verbs

- *This **might** be a problem.* (vs. "This is a problem.")
- *I **would say** the deadline is tight.*
- *It **seems** that the build failed.*
- *We **tend to** see this in production.*

## Hedge with adverbs

- **possibly, probably, arguably, somewhat, slightly, rather**
- *The results are **arguably** strong.*
- *We're **somewhat** behind schedule.*

## Hedge vs commit — the balance

- Too much hedging sounds unsure: *It kind of might possibly be an issue.*
- No hedging sounds blunt: *This is wrong.*
- Aim for **one hedge per claim** in sensitive messages.

## Register — matching the audience

| Situation | Style |
|---|---|
| Report to the board | formal, hedged, passive |
| Email to a colleague | neutral |
| Slack with the team | casual, direct |

- Formal: *It is recommended that the rollout be delayed.*
- Neutral: *I'd suggest delaying the rollout.*
- Casual: *Let's push the rollout.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| It **might** be an issue. | It might **is** an issue. (no verb form after modal) |
| **I'd argue** it's fine. | I'm arguing it's fine. (hedging ≠ stating) |
| We're **slightly** behind. | We're behind slightly much. |

## Quick check

- *The change ___ cause a regression.* → **might**
- *We're ___ behind schedule.* → **somewhat**
- *It ___ that the tests passed.* → **seems**

Softening is a skill: one hedge per claim, matched to the audience. Formal needs it; casual doesn't.
