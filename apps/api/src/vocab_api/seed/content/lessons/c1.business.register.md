# Business register

Register is the **volume knob** of professional English. The same idea can be
*We need money* or *We are seeking additional funding* — and the right choice
depends on the audience and channel.

## Formality ladder

| Casual (chat) | Neutral (email) | Formal (report/board) |
|---|---|---|
| Let's fix this. | We should address this. | It is recommended that we address this. |
| Can you check? | Could you review? | Please review at your earliest convenience. |
| We're late. | We are behind schedule. | The project has fallen behind its timeline. |
| I want more time. | I'd like an extension. | We respectfully request an extension. |

## Formal emails — the skeleton

- Opening: *Dear… / I hope this finds you well.*
- Purpose: *I am writing to…*
- Body: hedged, passive where neutral: *The matter has been reviewed.*
- Closing: *I look forward to your reply. / Best regards,*

## Meetings vs reports

- **Meetings**: neutral, direct, interactive: *What's the status? / Let's move on.*
- **Reports**: formal, passive, hedged: *It has been determined that…*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| **We are seeking** funding. | We are wanting money. (unidiomatic) |
| **Please review** at your convenience. | Please review when convenient to you. (wordy) |
| **I am writing to** confirm… | I am writing that… |

## Quick check

- *Formal: We ___ additional funding.* → **are seeking**
- *Neutral: ___ you review the draft?* → **Could**
- *Report: It has been ___ that the schedule is at risk.* → **determined**
- *Casual: ___ push it live.* → **Let's**

Match the channel to the register: chat → casual, email → neutral, report → formal. The idea stays the same; the costume changes.
