# Participle clauses: -ing and -ed

Participle clauses are a compact way to add information: *Walking in, I saw the
board.* They replace full clauses and are common in writing and tech docs.

## Active (-ing)

*Waiting for the build, I checked the tests.*
→ *While/As I was waiting for the build, …*

## Passive (-ed / V3)

*Written in Go, the service scales well.*
→ *Because it is written in Go, …*

## One subject rule — the golden rule

The participle's **hidden subject must be the main clause subject**:

- ✅ *Arriving late, I missed the demo.* (I arrived; I missed)
- ❌ *Arriving late, the demo had started.* (the demo did not arrive!)

## When to use

1. **Replace a relative clause**: *The dev **leading** the project is new.* (who leads)
2. **Reason**: ***Being** short on time, we skipped lunch.*
3. **Time**: ***After finishing** the review, we shipped.*
4. **Result**: *The config was wrong, **causing** a crash.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| Walking in, **I** saw… | Walking in, **the room** was dark. (subject mismatch) |
| Being tired, **she** left. | Being tired, **the call** was cut short. (who was tired?) |
| After submitting, **we** waited. | After submitting, **the form** closed. |

## Quick check

- *___ on time, the train left.* → **Pulling in** (the train pulls in) — or keep subject match.
- *The service, ___ in Rust, is fast.* → **written**
- *___ the review, she pressed ship.* → **Finishing** / **After finishing**

The participle points at the subject. If they don't match, the sentence breaks — rephrase with a full clause.
