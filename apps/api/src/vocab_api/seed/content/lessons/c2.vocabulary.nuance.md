# Lexical nuance

At C2 the gap between a good sentence and a precise one is **word choice**.
Near-synonyms diverge in connotation (what the word feels like), register (who
says it), and collocation (what it is allowed to touch).

## Connotation: the feeling of the word

| Neutral | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| thin | slim | skinny |
| confident | assertive | aggressive |
| unusual | original | odd |

*She is **slim**.* (admiring) vs *She is **skinny**.* (critical). Same body,
different judgment.

## Precision: shades of meaning

- **Talk** is general; **argue** implies conflict; **discuss** implies a
  structured exchange.
- **Refuse** is firm; **decline** is polite; **reject** is blunt.
- *The economy slowed* vs *the economy **stalled*** — the second suggests it
  almost stopped.

## Register: formality

| Formal | Neutral |
|---|---|
| purchase | buy |
| commence | start |
| obtain | get |
| approximately | about |

*We purchased the software* (contract) vs *We bought the software* (chat).

## Collocation: the word's neighbours

- **Heavy** traffic, **heavy** rain — but **strong** coffee, **strong** smell.
- **make** a decision / **do** a favour / **give** a speech.
- *Mitigate* collocates with *risk, damage* — not *with hunger*.

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| strong **coffee** | heavy coffee |
| she looks **slim** | she looks skinny (if the tone is positive) |
| We **declined** the offer. | We refused the offer. (fine — but declined is polite in this context) |

## Quick check

- *___ traffic* (not *strong*) → **heavy**
- The polite way to say *refuse an invitation*: → **decline**
- *The talks ___ down* (stopped almost completely) → **stalled**
