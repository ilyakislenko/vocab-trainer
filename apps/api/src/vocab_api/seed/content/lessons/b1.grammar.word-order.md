# Word order: subject, verb, object, adverbs

English is a **fixed-word-order** language: Subject → Verb → Object. Move the
pieces and you change the meaning or break the sentence.

## The skeleton

`Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time`

- *She writes reports at home in the morning.*
- Time often moves to the front for emphasis: *In the morning, she writes reports.*

## Adverbs of frequency — a fixed slot

`always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never` sit **before the main verb**
but **after `be`**:

- *He **often** fixes bugs.* (before main verb)
- *He is **often** on call.* (after be)

## Questions — inversion

- **Yes/no**: *Is he ready?* — `be`/auxiliary first, then subject.
- **Wh-**: *Where do you work?* — question word + auxiliary + subject + main verb.
- With `be`: *Where **is** the office?* — no auxiliary needed.

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| I **always** arrive early. | I arrive **always** early. |
| **Do** you speak English? | You speak English? (needs auxiliary) |
| She is **usually** late. | She usually is late. (frequency after be) |
| I read books **every day**. | I read every day books. |

## Adverbs of manner and place

- Manner (how) comes after the object: *She wrote the code **carefully**.*
- Place before time: *He waited **at the door** **for an hour**.*

## Quick check

- *She ___ goes to the gym.* (usually) → **usually goes** (before main verb)
- *___ you like coffee?* → **Do**
- *He reads newspapers ___.* (every morning) → **every morning** (at the end)

Order is grammar in English. Keep SVO rigid, pin adverbs to their slot, and invert only for questions.
