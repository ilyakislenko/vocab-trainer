# Cleft sentences for emphasis

A cleft sentence **splits one sentence into two** to spotlight a single element.
Native speakers reach for it when a plain sentence doesn't put the emphasis
where the speaker wants it.

## The it-cleft

`It + be + FOCUS + who/that + rest`

- Plain: *The team fixed the bug.*
- Focus on the team: **It was the team who fixed the bug.**
- Focus on the bug: **It was the bug that the team fixed.**
- Focus on the time: **It was on Friday that they fixed it.**

## The wh-cleft

`What/All + be + FOCUS`

- *What we need is more testing.*
- *What surprised me was how fast it shipped.*
- *All I want is a quiet release.*

## When to use

Use clefts in speech and writing to **correct a misunderstanding** or to
**contract a long explanation**:

- *I didn't say the design was bad. **What I said** was that we needed more time.*

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| It **was** the team **who** fixed it. | It was the team fixed it. (missing who) |
| **What** I meant was… | That I meant was… (wh-word required) |
| It's the **deadline** that matters. | It's the deadline matters. |

## Quick check

- *___ was the deadline that mattered.* → **It**
- *___ surprised me was the speed.* → **What**
- *It was the CTO ___ approved it.* → **who**

Pick one element, build the shell around it (*It was X who… / What X is…*), and the emphasis lands exactly where you want it.
