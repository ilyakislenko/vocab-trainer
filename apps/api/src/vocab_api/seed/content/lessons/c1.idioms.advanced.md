# Advanced idioms

Idioms compress **shared experience** into a few words. In business they add
colour and fluency — but overuse, or use in the wrong register, undermines you.

## Negotiation and deals

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| get down to brass tacks | discuss the real details | Let's get down to brass tacks. |
| sweeten the deal | improve an offer | They sweetened the deal. |
| ballpark figure | rough estimate | Give me a ballpark figure. |
| drive a hard bargain | negotiate firmly | She drives a hard bargain. |
| on the table | available to discuss | The offer is still on the table. |
| up in the air | undecided | The budget is still up in the air. |

## Outcomes and risk

- **a win-win situation** — both sides benefit.
- **a risky business** — a venture with risk.
- **hit the ground running** — start effectively.
- **a moving target** — something that keeps changing.

## Register — when idioms fit

- ✅ Negotiations, internal chats, casual meetings.
- ❌ Formal contracts, board reports, legal text.
- Use **one idiom per topic**; a string of idioms sounds clichéd.

## Common mistakes

| Correct | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| Let's get **down to** brass tacks. | Let's get to the brass tacks. |
| It's a **win-win** situation. | It's a win-win-win situation. (usually binary) |
| Give me a **ballpark figure**. | Give me a ballpark number estimate. |

## Strategy

Learn idioms in **situational sets** (negotiation, risk, planning). For each,
know its *one* trigger moment — the exact meeting moment where it lands. Use it
there, not everywhere.

## Quick check

- *Can you give me a ___ figure?* → **ballpark**
- *The decision is still ___ in the air.* → **up**
- *Let's get down to ___ tacks.* → **brass**
- *It's a ___ situation for both teams.* → **win-win**

An idiom is a precision tool, not a decoration. Use it in the right meeting moment and it buys you fluency.
